# Do event odds lead the perp? — 22 September 2026

## Why this was measured

The board shows zero candidates. `isActionable` requires `timing.status === "odds-leads"`, and production never reports it: the liquid markets come back "simultaneous" and the illiquid ones "weak-or-flat-series". At the collector's one-minute cadence that single observation is consistent with two very different worlds — the lead is faster than a minute, or there is no lead — and minute bars cannot separate them.

## Method

`scripts/lead-lag-probe.ts` (`npm run research:lead-lag 90`), revision `3403d9c`, 08:00–09:30 UTC. Public endpoints only, no writes: the Polymarket CLOB book for each event's Yes token and the venue's perp tickers, sampled every 5 s. 1,080 polls, zero failures, 16 pairs.

For each pair it computes the same cross-correlation the collector does — odds change against perp return — at lags of ±60 s in 5 s steps, then repeats it on the identical series aggregated to 60 s bars. A pair counts only after 120 samples with 30 actual odds changes: scanning 25 lags on a mostly flat series will always produce a high correlation, and a 2-minute smoke run duly "found" a lead on a market whose odds had moved four times.

## Result

Eleven of sixteen pairs had enough movement to measure. **No pair showed a lead at any resolution.** The co-movement is strong on minute bars and buried in noise at 5 s:

| Market | odds changes | 5 s: best lag / r | 60 s: best lag / r |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BTC above $86,000 | 909 | 5 s / 0.21 | **0 s / 0.73** |
| BTC above $84,000 | 807 | 0 s / 0.12 | 0 s / 0.60 |
| ETH above $2,800 | 673 | 0 s / 0.17 | **0 s / 0.80** |
| BTC above $84,000 | 625 | 0 s / 0.13 | 0 s / 0.64 |
| ETH above $2,800 | 585 | 0 s / 0.20 | 0 s / 0.70 |
| Gold (HIGH) $4,400 | 245 | 0 s / 0.16 | 0 s / 0.54 |
| SOL above $120 | 141 | 0 s / 0.06 | 0 s / 0.48 |
| WTI, Silver, HYPE, PUMP | 60–148 | ≤0.08, no stable lag | ≤0.28, no stable lag |

At 5 s both series are dominated by microstructure noise — odds tick quantisation and mark jitter — so the relationship is swamped. Aggregating to 60 s averages that away and the co-movement appears, at lag zero. That ordering (weak at 5 s, strong at 60 s, always centred on zero) is the signature of two prices moving together, not one leading the other.

## Interpretation

This is expected once the eligibility rule is taken literally. `SIGNAL_POLICY.eligibility` is `own-price-threshold-v1`, and `priceEligibility` rejects anything that is not a threshold on the perp's own price (`non-price-event`, `relative-performance`, `multiple-underlyings`). "Will Bitcoin be above $86,000" **is a function of the Bitcoin price**: the market prices it off the same underlying the perp quotes. Its odds cannot lead that price, because they are derived from it. The residual such a market leaves is a pricing discrepancy between two venues plus spread noise — not foreknowledge.

The events where a lead is plausible — a recession call, an OPEC decision, a delisting, an earnings surprise — resolve on information outside the price, and those are exactly the questions eligibility refuses. **So the product as currently gated cannot find what it is looking for**: the markets where the implied-move maths is rigorous are the markets where no lead can exist, and the markets where a lead is possible are excluded.

## What this does not establish

- 90 minutes of one quiet pre-US-open session. A lead could appear in a volatility shock (a CPI print, an unexpected headline) and be invisible here. Re-run during a scheduled event before treating "no lead" as settled.
- It says nothing about non-price events, which were never sampled because none are eligible.
- Correlation at lag zero does not bound the *size* of the cross-venue discrepancy; the ten rows unblocked by the fee fix showed gaps of 0.06–1.33% against costs of 0.08–0.12%.

## Options this leaves

1. **Reframe threshold markets as cross-venue pricing divergence.** Drop the odds-leads requirement for them, keep every cost and evidence check, and describe a qualifying row as "the event market and the perp disagree by more than it costs to trade", which is what the data supports. Honest, and the board would populate.
2. **Re-admit non-price events and measure them.** The mapping corpus already covers macro, oil, semiconductor and crypto-equity clusters. Run this probe against those pairs to find out whether a lead exists where it is at least possible, and accept the coarser sizing that non-threshold events require.
3. **Leave v5 as is** and accept a board that is empty except during genuine dislocations.

Option 1 changes the product's claim; option 2 tests the original claim where it could be true. Neither should be taken by relaxing the timing gate while keeping the "odds lead" framing.
